    <section class="px-band px-band-sun px-band-sun-gold px-band-sun-left px-grad-soft">
      <div class="max-w-2xl mx-auto px-6 lg:px-10 py-20 md:py-28">
        <p class="px-label text-[#B0812F] mb-4">The thinking behind it</p>
        <h2 class="px-display text-4xl md:text-6xl mb-8">Why Praxis Ecology</h2>
        <p class="px-body-lg text-[#3B241C]">
          Most intentional communities collapse after a couple of years, most religious communities succeed over centuries. Could we find the middle way to get what works in religious communities while still having some agency with our personal life? Could we benefit from the deep listening of contemplation while leaving our life connected to web information?
        </p>
        <p class="mt-6 px-body-lg text-[#3B241C]">
          This is the exploration of the Praxis way of living, inspired by both zen monachism and lay spiritual communities.
        </p>
        <!-- The situation -->
        <h3 class="px-subhead px-label text-[#B0812F] mt-20 mb-10">The situation</h3>
        <p class="px-quote text-[#C2251D]">
          Every ending carries the seeds of its transformation
        </p>
        <p class="mt-8 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
          Even if we do not yet fully feel it, others — many humans and non-human animals, vegetal and mineral worlds — have been experiencing it for a long time. The effects of the old world culture built on separation are gradually permeating and overwhelming us.
        </p>
        <p class="mt-5 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
          Individualist western culture has been essential to a certain kind of human development. But it has reached its limits. Its expression — extractive, polarized, and self-centered — has contributed to a world shaped by scarcity and violence, where, together, we are slowly eroding the very conditions that sustain life in this world.
        </p>
        <p class="mt-5 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
          Every system carries within it both its limits and the seeds of its transformation. These cycles follow a rhythm: what can no longer hold gives way, not only in collapse but in opening — making space for something new to emerge.
        </p>
        <p class="mt-5 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
          In this sense, what we are experiencing is both an ending and a beginning — a threshold where we can bear witness to the emergence of new forms within the structures of the old — the seeds of a Second Renaissance.
        </p>
        <div class="mt-12 border-l-2 border-[#C9A961] pl-7">
          <p class="px-label text-[#B0812F] mb-5">A diagnosis</p>
          <p class="px-body text-[#3B241C]">
            We are living in a moment between worlds — this transition has been described as a period of civilizational crisis and potential rebirth, where old assumptions are breaking down and new ways of being are gestating.
          </p>
          <p class="mt-5 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
            We are shaped, conditioned, by the old world and have internalized its cultural paradigm. We carry individualist habits, not only in thought but in action and feeling. So we cannot simply think our way into a new way of living just by thinking about it — we must design containers that produce new ways of being in order to act differently in the world.
          </p>
        </div>
        <!-- Where we begin -->
        <h3 class="px-subhead px-label text-[#B0812F] mt-20 mb-10">Where we begin</h3>
        <p class="px-display text-3xl sm:text-4xl text-[#3B241C] leading-snug">
          A new way of acting emerges from bearing witness, not from solving. We can practice this posture in conscious communities, in secular monasteries.
        </p>
        <p class="mt-8 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
          Praxis Ecology begins on this ground. We carry individualist habits, not only in thought but in action and feeling — so we cannot simply think our way into a new way of living. Instead, we begin where we are:
        </p>
        <ul class="mt-8 space-y-4 list-none pl-0">
          <li class="flex gap-4 items-baseline"><span class="text-[#C2251D] font-bold text-xl">&rarr;</span><span class="px-display text-3xl text-[#3B241C]">We sit with the trouble.</span></li>
          <li class="flex gap-4 items-baseline"><span class="text-[#C2251D] font-bold text-xl">&rarr;</span><span class="px-display text-3xl text-[#3B241C]">We stay with what is here, as it is.</span></li>
          <li class="flex gap-4 items-baseline"><span class="text-[#C2251D] font-bold text-xl">&rarr;</span><span class="px-display text-3xl text-[#3B241C]">We organize around it, as we are.</span></li>
        </ul>
        <p class="mt-8 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
          From there, another way of relating can emerge — not from aspiration alone, but from interactions. We approach the world through daily collective movement, and in that shared life our ways of feeling and acting transform naturally.
        </p>
        <p class="mt-5 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
          Rather than thinking our way through community building, this proposition is grounded in a different way of relating — one that reshapes how we connect with one another. It involves an ongoing oscillation between self-expression and collective service — an interplay that expands our sense of self and allows new ways of thinking to emerge. This is both a praxis that responds to uncertainty, and one that has the capacity to rewrite its narrative through the joy of being alive together.
        </p>
        <!-- Not a method -->
        <h3 class="px-subhead px-label text-[#B0812F] mt-20 mb-10">Not a method — a living structure</h3>
        <p class="px-quote text-[#C2251D]">
          A fluid framework — a direction from which creativity can emerge
        </p>
        <p class="mt-8 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
          Praxis Ecology is not a model to apply, nor a step-by-step method to follow. It is a structure — a resilient container that supports collective life amid uncertainty and change.
        </p>
        <p class="mt-5 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
          In the spirit of a Second Renaissance — where profound shifts in how we think, feel, and act are both necessary and possible — this structure, grounded in zen tradition, helps groups to sit with what is here rather than constantly react, fix, or escape, and creates enough stability to foster belonging, respond to scarcity with peace, and cultivate response rather than reaction.
        </p>
        <p class="mt-5 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
          This is a direction. From it, each group is invited to integrate their own creativity — adapting to the different subcultures they're coming from, such as metamodernism, contemplative activism, secular buddhism, ecospirituality, deep ecology, and inner transition. You will be able to design your own secular monastery through a fluid framework that gives just enough shape to allow something genuinely alive to emerge.
        </p>
        <p class="mt-5 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
          Through simple, repeated forms — shared rhythms, roles, and daily practices — the group begins to move together. In that movement, something changes. Not because we solve everything, but because we are engaged in the dance of uncertainty together through daily rhythm and contact.
        </p>
        <div class="mt-12 bg-white/65 rounded-2xl p-8 border border-[#EBD8C0]">
          <p class="px-display text-3xl sm:text-4xl text-[#3B241C] mb-6">You are designing a secular monastery</p>
          <p class="px-body text-[#3B241C]">
            A framework and a set of practices that integrate both the visible and the invisible, the measurable and the unmeasurable, the past and the future.
          </p>
          <p class="mt-5 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
            A container to cultivate resilience — responding to the situation both emotionally (dealing with our paradoxes, conditioning, shadow patterns) and contextually (constraints, potential scarcity):
          </p>
          <ul class="mt-5 space-y-2 px-body text-[#3B241C] list-disc pl-5 m-0">
            <li>Realistic in what it can hold — not too much, but enough</li>
            <li>Grounded in the reality of daily life</li>
            <li>Able to sustain engagement, energy, and continuity over time</li>
            <li>Celebrating the joy of life every day</li>
          </ul>
        </div>
        <!-- Why this matters -->
        <h3 class="px-subhead px-label text-[#B0812F] mt-20 mb-10">Why this matters</h3>
        <p class="px-body text-[#3B241C]">
          We know we need to expand from personal to transpersonal self to adapt to the new conditions of our world. We feel the limits of individualism and look at community as a direction. At the same time, attempts to build collective ways of living often struggle with:
        </p>
        <div class="mt-8 grid grid-cols-1 sm:grid-cols-2 gap-6 lg:-mx-16">
          <div class="bg-white/65 rounded-2xl p-7 border border-[#EBD8C0]">
            <p class="px-label text-[#B0812F] mb-5">Often struggle with</p>
            <ul class="space-y-2 px-body text-[#3B241C] list-disc pl-5 m-0">
              <li>Lack of structure</li>
              <li>Unrealistic expectations</li>
              <li>Difficulty sustaining engagement over time</li>
              <li>Fragmentation in daily coordination</li>
              <li>Exhaustion</li>
            </ul>
          </div>
          <div class="px-grad text-white rounded-2xl p-7">
            <p class="px-label text-[#F6E7C8] mb-5">Praxis Ecology starts from</p>
            <ul class="space-y-2 px-body text-white/95 list-disc pl-5 m-0">
              <li>Shared constraints</li>
              <li>Human patterns and conditioning</li>
              <li>The necessity of food, coordination, and rhythm</li>
              <li>The friction of living together</li>
            </ul>
          </div>
        </div>
        <p class="mt-6 px-body text-[#6B5245] italic">
          Praxis Ecology approaches this differently. Instead of starting from ideals, it starts from reality.
        </p>
        <p class="mt-10 px-display text-3xl sm:text-4xl text-[#3B241C] leading-snug">
          Transformation is not treated as a goal to achieve, but as something that emerges when the conditions are there to activate it.
        </p>
        <!-- What it is / is not -->
        <h3 class="px-subhead px-label text-[#B0812F] mt-20 mb-10">What this course is — and is not</h3>
        <div class="grid grid-cols-1 sm:grid-cols-2 gap-6 lg:-mx-16">
          <div class="bg-white/65 rounded-2xl p-7 border border-[#EBD8C0]">
            <p class="px-label text-[#8A7A6B] mb-5">What it is not</p>
            <p class="px-body text-[#3B241C]">
              This is not about understanding or trying to find solutions to make people live in harmony through dialogue or communication. It is not about healing trauma, shared governance systems, economic models, or political vision.
            </p>
            <p class="mt-5 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
              We start from where we are, within our limitations — and we believe that creating spaces where people agree to follow a shared structure and schedule has a strong impact on harmony.
            </p>
          </div>
          <div class="bg-white rounded-2xl p-7 border-2 border-[#C9A961]">
            <p class="px-label text-[#B0812F] mb-5">What it is</p>
            <p class="px-body text-[#3B241C]">
              A radical posture that takes as its starting point our incapacity to live together under common values, due to collective and personal trauma that may take generations to heal.
            </p>
            <p class="mt-5 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
              Inspired by the structural aspects of monastic communities and the practice of resisting individual impulses — balancing a weekly collective framework with space for private time.
            </p>
            <p class="mt-5 px-body text-[#3B241C]">
              It is about organizing ourselves through a system that distributes responsibilities, based on the belief that personal engagement in a collective movement already helps to reduce tension.
            </p>
          </div>
        </div>
      </div>
