Across the field(s) of thought and practice dedicated to cultural transition, wisdom is a word we often invoke, but seldom examine. Wisdom means many things to many people - from a loose sense of shrewd or intuitive knowing, to a vast array of traditions and practices designed and evolved towards living a good life. In the first of two companion essays, we spend some time getting to know wisdom, considering its scarcity within the current, dominant cultural paradigm, and what it might mean as a foundational principle of a healthier future society. A second paper exploring the spiritual dimension of wisdom is forthcoming.

## Summary

Modernity has staged a heist: the field of legitimate knowledge has been stripped of all that’s good and beautiful, leaving only what can be measured. No wonder we’ve forgotten to trust our inner compass - what use is wisdom when the only admissible civic choices concern how much and many? Neglected and under-cultivated, our innate faculty of wisdom is weak, but it isn’t extinct. Our future depends on reviving it. 

Against a backdrop of global crisis, wisdom emerges as a vital capacity for a regenerative future society. Where wisdom can be framed as a capacity for choosing what’s good and acting effectively, its foundations include discernment of extra-rational, unmeasurable value. Modern systems, shaped by reductive materialism, have neglected value and dulled the inner compass that knows (and co-creates) it, with consequences all the more catastrophic given our technological power to harm what is most precious in the living world. Closing this potentially deadly ‘wisdom gap’ depends on reintegrating intuitive capacities with other powerful ways of knowing, and empowering decisionmakers through wisdom-preserving practices.
